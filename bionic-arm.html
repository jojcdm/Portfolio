01 / Engineering Challenge

Compact mechanical packaging on a moving forearm

Translating natural wrist flexion into reliable mechanical energy release requires tight control over linkage friction, spring retention, ergonomic forearm fit, and part-to-part FDM dimensional repeatability.

Rather than relying on monolithic glued shells, I architected the assembly as 60+ interchangeable components so worn latches, trigger profiles, and launch barrels could be refined and swapped independently across rapid print-test cycles.

02 / Mechanical Subsystems

Architecture Breakdown

01

Wrist-Flexion Linkage

Mechanical transmission converting wrist articulation into crisp trigger actuation without interfering with normal hand movement.

02

Modular Shell & Fastening

Interchangeable 3D-printed subassemblies engineered with controlled print orientations to align tensile load along filament extrusion lines.

03

Tolerance Stack Iteration

Over 500 hours of empirical fit tuning across sliding pins, pivots, and spring guides to achieve smooth, jam-free operation straight off the build plate.
